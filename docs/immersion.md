# Interface motion and material

The title is a view through station glass: a dark metal rim, a visibly rotating Earth with independent cloud drift, an authored maintenance relay orbiting the planet, and a small response to mouse position. The relay follows a world-space orbit: its apparent size changes with distance, it faces away from Earth, and the planet occludes it on the far side. The title lettering resolves in sequence. Current travels around the primary control and through the small status line. These effects give the player something to perceive after the initial fade has finished, without adding instructions or telemetry panels.

The notebook is a physical tablet raised into the player's view. Its case has a recessed display, edge thickness, fasteners, camera, speaker and side keys. The lower bezel is unmarked, and the screen has a plain Notebook heading. A compact close icon replaces the chamber number in the upper-right screen header. There is no Return footer or lower-bezel power key. Closing retains the screen-off/lowering sequence. The case stays opaque throughout travel, while the screen illuminates as the tablet rises; closing dims the display and lowers the device. The display uses the same uppercase sans-serif headings and pale selection block as the pause menu. Parts, Formulas and Map remain text-only tabs. Five monochrome hardware pictograms sit above their labels in a three-column grid, reduced to two columns on phones. Short descriptions, uniform strokes and centered polarity marks keep the parts readable. Formulas use simple dividers and the map shares the neutral display palette. Tab selection replaces the page with a short transition. The map has a locator pulse and flow through the restored route.

## Behavior and input

The walking HUD has only the chamber marker, reticle and contextual bench interaction. N opens the notebook from walking or a circuit; Escape pauses while walking. At a bench, Back, the room number/name and a tablet-shaped notebook control share one compact header. The activity has no completion badge or progress bars. Its inventory tray shows rendered kit parts, unlimited wire and remaining part counts, alongside Undo and Reset. A short placement hint appears only while parts are available to add; faults retain their repair guidance. Closing a modal restores its previous control, with the circuit controls or world canvas as the fallback focus target.

Response takes priority: the opening can be interrupted by the header close button, Escape, N or J. Closing captures the current transform, opacity and display aperture, so interrupting entry does not snap the device to its resting pose. Repeated close commands share one completion. Keyboard events handled by the device stop before reaching the game. Native dialog close requests also reconcile React state, avoiding an invisible modal with a pending animation.

The native modal owns focus while the device is present. Closing returns focus to its opener and resumes the prior activity, including an unfinished circuit. A small relay tone accompanies opening/closing and a shorter tone accompanies changing tabs. Sound preferences apply to all of them. Both the saved preference and live operating-system reduced-motion setting stop presentation motion, including portal-rendered dialogs; reducing motion during closing finishes the close immediately.

Clarity comes from the familiar parts, three tabs and the header close icon. Satisfaction comes from coordinated movement, screen illumination and quiet audio. The shared metal/glass treatment ties the title, pause panel and notebook to the station. Discovered content and restored routes remain the motivation; there are no new unlock requirements or lesson steps.

## Starting values and checks

All visual dimensions, colors, poses, amplitudes and durations in this pass are starting values, not externally established constants.

## Cost and review

The tablet and its reference illustrations use DOM/CSS/SVG. Activity tray images are small transparent PNGs rendered from the playable kit by `scripts/render-kit-previews.mjs`; they add no runtime WebGL context and also work in the flat fallback. The station renderer holds a static frame behind menus. The title relay uses shared geometry and 32 instanced solar cells; it adds no lights or shadows and is hidden during play. Existing DPR limits, cached shadows and title post-processing exclusions remain in place. Planet rotation accumulates elapsed simulation time so switching between title and walking does not jump its orientation.

Run `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/review-immersion.mjs` against the review server for the recorded sequence and an intermediate tablet pose. Add `--orbit` to record a full title orbit. `scripts/profile-station.mjs` includes the moving title and tablet transitions as well as walking and circuit dragging. `tests/browser/immersion.spec.ts` covers interrupting entry, repeat close inputs, reopening, focus restoration, live reduced motion and notebook accessibility.

HUD/orbit verification (2026-09-12): the production build and nine focused browser scenarios passed, including the first repair, discovered notebook content, N/Escape access, rapid tablet dismissal/reopening, focus restoration to the world and circuit controls, live motion preferences, touch circuit controls, WebGL fallback and saved progress. The notebook accessibility scan reported no WCAG 2 A/AA violations. A full desktop orbit recording confirmed continuous foreground travel, occultation behind Earth and return. Production Chromium/Metal samples at 1280 × 720 measured 16.7 ms median and p90 frame intervals, with a 16.8 ms maximum, across the animated title, walking, circuit dragging and tablet transitions (120 measured frames per sample). These local intervals match the preceding pass; they do not establish the GPU budget or performance on school devices. Raw samples are in `artifacts/station/performance.json`.

The header close control uses the same dismissal path as N and Escape, preserving animated closing, reopening, focus restoration and reduced motion. Review captures and the motion script use this control.

New-player/readability check: find Begin, open the notebook, identify a familiar part and return without instruction. Stress/abuse check: hold/repeat dismissal keys, close during entry, switch the system motion preference during a transition and ensure no gameplay advances while a modal is active. Skill check: use keyboard shortcuts to consult the map and resume a circuit without touching the pointer.

**ASSUMPTION:** Raising a physical-looking device feels more coherent with the station than a flat panel. **IMPACT:** Motion and materials should improve the sense of inhabiting the station. **IF WRONG:** The animation feels decorative or becomes a delay. **VALIDATE:** Watch first-time and returning players open it several times; reduce travel and duration if they wait for it or repeatedly dismiss it before reading. Device-level performance and player preference still require testing on representative school hardware.
