# High-school playtest

Use a short observed session to check the spaceship premise, navigation, and the learning task. Automation does not substitute for these observations.

1. Show the title without explanation. Ask what the player expects to do and why the ship needs repairs.
2. Let the player find Engineering and open its console. Watch whether the HUD, deck map, and physical signage make the route clear.
3. Before repairing, ask what the auxiliary repair will change in the ship. Observe the first socket connection and material choice. Ask what the polymer and copper inserts change in the actual circuit.
4. Approach a sealed bulkhead, then restore its feed and return. Check whether the visual and sound changes explain the release. At the Power relay, ask for a prediction before disconnecting A. Listen for whether the player identifies a broken shared path.
5. Enter Command and watch the observation shield reveal. At Command, let a failed series backup reveal its consequence. Record whether the player can revise the circuit and explain B's independent path.
6. Ask the player to interpret the source and load readings; compare 3 V / 0.25 A in series with 6 V / 0.50 A in parallel. Watch the distress transmission and ask whether repairing the systems felt consequential. Ask which room or interaction they remember.
7. Check comfort with the camera, bloom, and transitions. Try reduced motion, mute, touch controls, and the safe return to Engineering.
8. Give a differently arranged circuit later to check transfer. Revisit it on another day to distinguish recall from immediately completing the game.

Before expanding the campaign, compare the ship concept with the preserved lighthouse for a small group of actual high-school players. Track willingness to continue, independent navigation, successful reasoning, and frustration points.
