# Student playtest protocol

Use the [research and design report](research/station-design.md) as the instructional specification. Automated tests verify electrical behavior and interaction reliability; they cannot establish that students learned the concepts.

Observe a high-school learner who has met charge and potential but has not seen the station. Ask them to restore it without describing the interface. Watch whether the inset service display and nearby E prompt are enough to identify the first console without a cabinet banner or floor label. During the first repair, record whether they identify the loose connection, test without searching for a prediction form, and notice the lighting change. After the hub opens, ask which two repairs are available. Watch whether the short doorway names, landmarks and map are sufficient to choose a wing. Check that the compact objective stays readable in a restored room with bright fixtures.

For the specimen, ask what they intend to hold constant before a second reading. After the pump repair, ask where the source's energy goes. At the junction, ask what changes when a parallel branch is isolated and what remains unchanged. At the capacitor bank, ask what the displayed charge describes. At the airlock, ask whether the capacitor's voltage changes instantaneously when the source disconnects and what a larger RC changes. Ask these as observation prompts, not additional software unlock conditions.

Use unseen-circuit tasks before play, immediately after play and after a delay. Include unequal resistor branches, a changed source resistance, unequal capacitor modules and a qualitative RC graph. Score the explanation and representation separately from arithmetic. Include a physical-meter follow-up for measurement uncertainty. Report the number and preparation of learners, conditions and individual results before making a learning-gain claim.

Starting acceptance values: a new learner should identify the next available action in each panel without facilitator intervention on at least four of the five advanced instruments. If this fails, first simplify the labels, feedback or visible control set. If learners finish by guessing but cannot explain a transfer circuit, revise the comparison prompts and consequences rather than adding a quiz gate.

Stress the interface separately: rapid tests, changed values after a successful result, reset, undo, closing during charging, hidden tabs, reloading, corrupted saves, narrow screens, denied pointer lock, unavailable audio voices and WebGL failure. Commissioned evidence must remain stable when experimenting in practice mode.
